# Crit canon

Thin by design. Everything here is **held as reference for humans to run, never as a procedure for the agent to perform** (`sheerline-skill-buildout-research-2026-08-09.md` § 6.3: crit formats are reference for humans; model critique is never a final gate). The skill reads this to know what a pre-read must carry and what a capture must preserve. It does not read it to learn how to critique.

*Internal operational reference (staged under `skill-updates/`, deployed under `heyhi-skills/` on promotion): em-dash limit exempt per `OPERATING_PRINCIPLES.md`.*

*Source of record for this canon: the buildout research § 6.3 (the facilitation canon of record). The authors below (Connor & Irizarry, Greever, Minto, and the CritiqueCrew finding) are the ones that research names; book titles are added here for findability only. The crit-format list is widely documented practice and its origins are noted for orientation, not as load-bearing citations.*

## 1. The critique boundary (the fact the whole skill leans on)

**Critique evaluates work against stated goals, through dialogue.** (Connor & Irizarry, *Discussing Design*.) Feedback is a reaction; critique is an evaluation *against something*. That "something" is the goals the presenter states, and the dialogue is the room's.

Two structural consequences the skill enforces mechanically:

- **A pre-read with no stated goals is not a crit pre-read.** It is an invitation to feedback. The skill flags the missing goals and stops; it never drafts candidate goals, and it never derives them from the artifact (deriving the evaluation axis from the work is the first step of evaluating it).
- **The room's judgment is the crit.** The pre-read carries the material the room evaluates *against*; the capture carries what the room *decided*. Neither carries an evaluation. The canonical rule for that is authored once in `sheerline/os/operating-principles.md` § The zero-cost operating invariants ("Critique and facilitation governance") and is pointed at, not restated, here.

## 2. Model critique's place (stated so it cannot be misread as license)

The 2026 CritiqueCrew finding, per the research: a model critic is an always-available **first reviewer**, never a final gate, and human critique remains stronger. This skill is **not that first reviewer either**. It is the room's clerk. The finding is recorded here so a build session cannot read "first reviewer" as permission to add a verdict field to a clerk.

## 3. Crit formats (a reference list; the facilitator picks)

What each is for and what it needs from the pre-read. The skill shows this list; it does not choose from it.

| Format | What it is for | What the pre-read must carry |
|---|---|---|
| **Structured crit** (goals-first, round-robin) | The default: each critic speaks to the stated goals in turn; the presenter listens, then responds | the goals, the presenter's questions, a time-box per round |
| **Design-studio crit** (sketch → pitch → critique → iterate; Warfel / Evans lineage) | Early divergence across several directions; critique is against the shared brief, not one artifact | the shared brief as the goals; one link per direction; the iteration time-box |
| **Plussing** (Pixar lineage) | Keeping momentum: every critical point must come with a build ("and…"), never a bare "no" | the goals; a note that dispositions default `undecided` because plussing generates options, not decisions |
| **"I like / I wish / What if"** (Stanford d.school lineage) | Collects input in three fixed sentence frames so every voice contributes in the same shape | the goals; the three frames stated in the invite |
| **Rose / Thorn / Bud** (LUMA Institute lineage) | Sorts input into working / not-working / opportunity | the goals; a capture plan (the sort is the room's, the capture is the skill's) |
| **Silent / gallery crit** (written comments on posted work, then discussion) | Reducing anchoring on the loudest voice; large rooms | artifact links that render on their own; the render-and-look note matters most here |

The facilitator's pick is recorded in the pre-read only when the facilitator made it. The skill never recommends a format.

## 4. Generic question frames (shown unfilled)

When the presenter brings no questions, the pre-read shows these frames **unfilled** as a reference. The skill does not substitute a goal string into a frame, because a list of goal-shaped questions is a suggested agenda of what to look at (spec § 10, D3). The presenter writes the questions.

- Does the work achieve [a stated goal]? Where does it, and where does it not?
- Which of the stated goals is this work serving least, and is that a choice?
- What would a person who holds [a stated goal] need to see to be convinced?
- What is the one question the presenter most wants answered and has not asked?

## 5. Capture discipline

- **Their language is truth; the skill's is translation, and translation is minimized.** The `heyhi-workshop` Capture Rule, generalized. An item is recorded in the room's words. When the room says a direction "feels heavy," the capture says "feels heavy," not "visual weight concern."
- **A disposition moves only on a quoted settlement phrase.** The tells are borrowed from the decision-record keeper's canon (`heyhi-skills/design-rationale-decision-record/references/decision-record-canon.md` § Decision-verb / settlement-phrase heuristics): the full list, carried as the keeper states it: settlement phrases "we'll go with", "let's ship / drop / kill / cut", "decided to", "final call", "the call is", "agreed", "we're going with X (over Y)", "approved", "sign-off", "locked"; past-tense settlements "we ended up", "we went with", "we settled on", "in the end we"; reversal / supersession tells "actually, let's not", "changing course", "we're now", "instead of", "scrap that", "on second thought". A settlement phrase inside a hypothetical ("if we went with…"), a quote, or a question is **not** a settlement; the item stays `undecided`. A reversal tell marks an item for the supersede variant if it revisits a prior record.
- **Ownerless stays ownerless.** A follow-up the room did not assign is flagged ⚑, never assigned. A record with no stated Approver is left blank so the keeper's DACI-completeness check flags it downstream.
- **The coverage note.** State what source the capture came from and what was not captured. A capture from the note-taker's notes that stop at minute 45 says so. Absence of an item is only a finding if the source covered the whole session.

## 6. The articulation canon (a pointer only)

Greever (*Articulating Design Decisions*), Minto's SCQA (Situation, Complication, Question, Answer), and BLUF (bottom line up front) are the articulation canon the research names, with one caveat that decides how this skill uses them: **the struggle of articulating is the mechanism.** It is the same rule that keeps the decision-record keeper's Rationale slot empty. So the pre-read's *Framing* block is laid out in SCQA order as a form the presenter fills; the skill does not restructure the presenter's framing for them. Whether a restructure-for-audience pass ever ships is spec § 10 D2, recommended routed to the `heyhi-deck` / `heyhi-case-study` extension row, not here.
