# Diagnostic Workshops Reference

Five workshop formats for when the primary goal is diagnostic: naming what went wrong, understanding what it cost, and owning the consequences. These are the counterpart to the generative futurecasting workshops — both are required, neither is complete without the other.

Diagnosis without design is cynicism. Design without diagnosis is naivety. When a situation is primarily diagnostic, run these workshops. When the team is ready to design forward, see [workshop-facilitation.md](workshop-facilitation.md).

---

## Routing Guide

Use diagnostic workshops when the team's primary goal is to **name what's already wrong**, not design toward something new. Common indicators:
- "We know something is broken but we don't know what to call it"
- "People are burning out and we don't understand why"
- "Trust has broken down and we need to understand how"
- "The system is hurting the people it was supposed to help"

Use generative workshops ([workshop-facilitation.md](workshop-facilitation.md)) when:
- The team has done diagnostic work and is ready for "what now?"
- The goal is designing toward a preferred future
- A major decision needs to be stress-tested before being made

**The natural arc:** Diagnostic workshop → generative workshop. Any team that has run the Human-in-the-Loop workshop is ready for The Preferred Future. Any team that has run the Anti-Slop workshop is ready for Before You Build.

## Facilitator Notes

### What these conversations surface

These workshops touch burnout, displacement, complicity, skill loss, and professional identity. Participants will have real feelings. Some will be angry. Some will be relieved someone is finally naming it. Some will feel guilty about decisions they've made or systems they've built.

That's the point. The work only matters if it's honest. But honest doesn't mean reckless.

### Ground rules to set at the start

1. **This is not a blame exercise.** We're here to understand the system, not to point fingers at who built it.
2. **Discomfort is useful.** If nothing in this conversation makes you uncomfortable, we're not going deep enough.
3. **"I don't know" is a valid answer.** Especially to the question "Is this working?"
4. **What's said here stays here.** The specifics, not the insights. People can take the takeaways. The stories stay in the room.

### The three-question close (for every diagnostic workshop)

1. **What did we learn today that we didn't know (or didn't say) before?** Name the findings.
2. **What's one thing we should change in the next 30 days?** Not a roadmap. One thing. Small enough to actually happen. Concrete enough to measure.
3. **Who owns it?** A name. Not a team. Not "we all will." A person who will be accountable.

## Workshop 1: Human-in-the-Loop

*Designing systems where people stay in control.*

### Discussion prompts

**"Where did you used to make a decision that the system now makes for you?"** Be specific — track where the automation boundary has moved.

**"Which of those decisions are you glad you don't make anymore? Which ones do you miss?"** The first list is automation working. The second list is automation overreaching.

**"If the system failed tomorrow and you had to make every decision manually, which ones could you still make well?"** Surfaces skill atrophy in real time.

### Exercise: The Decision Audit

Each participant maps 5–10 decisions they're responsible for:
- Who actually makes this decision? (Me / The system / Shared / I don't know)
- Could I make this decision well without the system? (Yes / Probably / No / I'm not sure)
- Does someone own the outcome when this decision is wrong? (Yes, me / Yes, someone else / Nobody clearly)

The grid reveals where human-in-the-loop design is working and where it's broken. Share in pairs, discuss patterns as a group.

## Workshop 2: The Anti-Slop Workshop

*Reclaiming intentionality in how teams adopt and use intelligent systems.*

### Discussion prompts

**"How many tools does your team subscribe to? How many do they actually use with intention?"** The gap is a signal.

**"When was the last time someone on your team said 'I don't think we should use this' about a new system or tool? What happened?"** Surfaces whether there's cultural permission to push back.

**"What's the worst thing your team has shipped since adopting intelligent systems? Not a bug. The work that was technically correct and substantively empty."** The slop question. Names the thing most organizations know but don't discuss.

### Exercise: The Quality Honest Assessment (private)

- In the last month, how much of my team's output would I put in my portfolio? (All / Most / Some / Very little)
- Has the overall quality of our work gone up, down, or stayed the same since adopting new systems?
- Do I feel more or less proud of the work than I did a year ago?
- If a peer I respected reviewed our last three deliverables, what would they say?

No sharing required. The answers are the diagnosis.

## Workshop 3: Adaptive Organizations

*Structuring teams for meaningful work in a rapidly changing landscape.*

### Discussion prompts

**"If you removed every tool and system your team uses, what would your team be good at? What would they struggle with?"** Separates capability that lives in people from capability that lives in infrastructure.

**"Are your people organized around what they're good at, or around what the system needs them to do?"** Most teams designed their structure before their current tooling.

**"Who on your team has the most expertise and the least influence over decisions? Why?"** Surfaces the identity crisis symptom directly.

### Exercise: The Meaningful Work Map

Each participant divides their typical week:
- **Judgment work:** Requires my judgment, experience, or relationships
- **Bridge work:** A system could do this but I'm doing it because the system doesn't exist or isn't trusted
- **System maintenance work:** Doing alongside or because of a system: monitoring, approving, translating

If judgment work is less than 30%: the team structure is underusing them.
If system maintenance work is more than 40%: the infrastructure is consuming the people it was supposed to serve.

## Workshop 4: Craft at Scale

*How to maintain quality as you grow.*

### Discussion prompts

**"What's the last thing your team shipped that surprised you with how good it was? When was that?"** If people have to think hard or go back months, something is wrong.

**"Who on your team is the best at saying 'this isn't good enough'? What happens when they do?"** Surfaces whether there's institutional support for quality pushback.

**"Has your team gotten faster at producing work? Has the work gotten better?"** Most teams have an honest answer to both. The relationship between speed and quality is the finding.

### Exercise: The Craft Inventory

Rate the team's last 5–10 deliverables on two axes:
- **Speed:** How fast did this ship? (1–5)
- **Craft:** How proud is the team of the quality? (1–5)

Plot them. The quadrants tell a story. Most teams cluster in fast + not proud. That's the design problem.

## Workshop 5: Living Systems

*Building infrastructure that evolves with your organization.*

### Discussion prompts

**"Does your infrastructure serve your team, or does your team serve the infrastructure?"** This is the fundamental question.

**"When your system makes a decision you disagree with, what do you do?"** Options: override (healthy), escalate (bureaucratic), accept (resigned), don't notice (concerning).

**"If you could redesign one system from scratch with the principle that it needs to need your team's judgment to function, what would you change?"** People know where the infrastructure is failing them. This question gives them permission to articulate it.

### Exercise: The Infrastructure Relationship Audit

For each major system the team uses:
- Does this system make my team's judgment more or less important? (More / Same / Less)
- If this system broke, would my team know how to do the work without it? (Yes / Partially / No)
- Does someone on my team understand how this system makes its decisions? (Yes / Sort of / No)
- When this system is wrong, how long does it take us to notice? (Immediately / Hours / Days / We're not sure)

Systems scoring "less / no / no / we're not sure" are consuming the team instead of serving them.

## Transitioning from Diagnosis to Action

Every diagnostic workshop should end with a path forward. Recognition without action builds cynicism, not change.

The three-question close (see above) is non-negotiable. It converts the workshop from a naming exercise into an accountability moment.

When the team is ready to move from diagnosis to design — the next step is one of the three generative workshops in [workshop-facilitation.md](workshop-facilitation.md):
- Team that ran Human-in-the-Loop → The Preferred Future
- Team that ran Anti-Slop → Before You Build
- Any team that has named what's wrong → Signal Readers (to build ongoing early warning capacity)
